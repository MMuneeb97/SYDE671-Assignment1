Part 2: Advanced Processing & Analysis

Published on September 27, 2026 • By Muneeb

Welcome to Part 2 of the project. In this phase, we dive deeper into advanced image transformations and optimization algorithms. While Part 1 focused on fundamental channel alignments and preliminary baseline results, this article covers refined techniques to reduce edge noise and improve performance on high-resolution plates.

Figure 1: High-level architectural pipeline of the secondary transformation algorithm.

1. Pyramid Optimization & Edge Cropping

One major challenge with glass plate negatives is the presence of dark or damaged borders along the film margins. When computing Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC) across color channels, these border artifacts skew shift vectors and produce inaccurate alignment offsets.

To resolve this, we introduced dynamic border cropping before calculating the correlation matrix. By ignoring the outer 10%–15% margin of the array during shift evaluations, the correlation metric focuses strictly on high-frequency visual features inside the frame.

Figure 2A: Uncropped matrix with border artifacts.

Figure 2B: Dynamic margin cropping applied.

As illustrated above, cropping the outer border prevents false peaks in the NCC metric and guarantees clean spatial alignment even across multi-scale Gaussian pyramid levels.

2. Quantitative Results & Key Takeaways

Evaluating the revised pipeline across the entire dataset demonstrated significant runtime and accuracy improvements. Higher resolution plates that previously required extensive manual adjustments were aligned automatically in under 5 seconds per image.

Figure 3: Final reconstructed composite result.

In summary, combining slice-based array transformations with normalized metric evaluation provides a robust foundation for automated RGB glass plate restoration. Future work will explore automated color balancing and contrast adjustment.
